# Lore & Songs

*In-world tellings of Krynn's legends for Shadow of the Dragon Queen. My players didn't know the setting, so I used these to hand them the history without a lecture. Each one comes from a character with a reason to tell it their own way.*

---

## Fizban's Tale of Huma

> **When to use it.** Somewhere in or after Chapter 4, once the party reaches Kalaman. The idea of having Fizban tell Huma's story came from the community; the telling is mine.

> **GM note.** Fizban is Paladine in disguise, so his asides about Paladine are jokes at his own expense. Players who don't know the setting just hear a rambling old man. The payoff comes later.

Ah, yes, gather round, dear children... Huma Dragonbane! Sit down, sit down, you're in for quite a tale! Now, where was I? Ah, of course... Huma! A Knight of Solamnia, as brave as they come and twice as stubborn. Good lad, really. Very shiny armor, but you know, it's always the ones with the shiny armor who end up needing a polish by the end of their adventures.

So, Huma was born, oh, I'd say around the time when dragons still had their fiery breath set on roasting the whole of Krynn; pesky business, that was. This was during the Third Dragon War, mind you. Takhisis, the Dark Queen herself, was stomping around, trying to turn the world into her personal dragon buffet. Not a good situation for anyone involved, least of all for the people who didn't fancy being dragon snacks.

Ah, Huma... wanted to be a knight, but folks said he couldn't, because he was poor and his family wasn't important. Like many, he doubted himself, but after praying to Paladine, he found the courage he needed and realized greatness doesn't come from birth, but from heart. Oh yes, Paladine is real, though not all he's cracked up to be, if you know what I mean. Forgotten, perhaps, but as real as the nose on your face.

Now, in time, Huma became a Knight of the Crown. A real stickler for duty and honor and all those knightly things that make you want to give a speech before doing anything remotely dangerous. But here's the rub: Huma wasn't just any knight, no sir! He had that rarest of qualities: destiny! It followed him around like a lost puppy, always biting at his heels.

One day, out of the blue, well, more like out of a forest, Huma runs into this strange silver dragon. Now, don't look at me like that! Yes, a dragon, but not one of the bad ones! This was a good dragon, see? Turns out she could also shapeshift into a lovely woman, which, well, let's just say Huma's heart was no longer just committed to knighthood after that. Her name? Gwyneth. Rather on the nose, if you ask me!

They fought together, side by side, battling Takhisis' forces: dragons here, dragons there... dragons everywhere. The war was raging, and Huma, being the ever-stubborn knight, decided to march right up to Takhisis' door and give it a good knock. Well, metaphorically speaking. In reality, there were more dragons involved, much more fire, and a lot less knocking.

But here's the kicker! You see, Huma wasn't just out there flailing his sword around like some overly enthusiastic tourist. No, no, he was on a mission to find the one thing that could turn the tide: the Dragonlance! Ah, yes, the Dragonlance. A weapon forged with the power of the gods themselves, designed to bring dragons to their knees. You know, for a lance, it really was quite dramatic. It had flair, it had pizzazz! Anyway, with this magnificent lance in hand, Huma was able to face down Takhisis herself.

Picture it! Huma, standing tall, lance gleaming in the light, staring down the Dark Queen. She roared, she snarled, she did all those villainous things... yet Huma stood firm. Takhisis tried her best, but our dear Huma, with Gwyneth's help and a whole lot of divine intervention, forced her back into the Abyss! A real "go back to where you came from" moment, if I ever saw one.

And what happened after? Ah, well... Huma, the poor chap, gave his life to ensure Takhisis stayed locked away. The ultimate sacrifice, they say. But his story didn't end there! Oh no! The Knights of Solamnia continued his legacy, and the Dragonlances, those lovely, shiny, dragon-stabbing sticks, became symbols of hope and valor for generations to come.

So, dear children, if you ever find yourself facing down a dragon or, say, a god bent on world domination, just remember: be brave, find yourself a good lance, and always, *always* look for the silver dragon hiding in plain sight! Now, where did I put my tea...

## In Dargaard's Keep

> **When to use it.** At Ispin Greenshield's funeral reception in Vogler. The module has the mysterious Leedara sing "a heartbreakingly beautiful Silvanesti song in Elvish" if a character persuades her (DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion)), but never says what the song is. This is the song.

> **GM note.** Leedara is one of the elf maidens who traveled with Isolde, and in the legend, those maidens told Soth his wife was unfaithful. That lie turned him from his quest. So Leedara sings his story with no mercy for him and no mention of herself. She names his murders plainly, but the whisper that turned him back comes from no one ("Told his bride was false"). The song never says who told him.

**Running the song:**

- Leedara sings in Elvish. Characters who understand it hear the words below; everyone else hears only that the song is beautiful and very sad.
- **DC 15 Intelligence (History)**, for a character who understands Elvish and knows the legend: the song never says who told Soth his bride was false. In the tellings, it was the elf maidens traveling with Isolde.
- **DC 18 Wisdom (Insight)**: Leedara's voice catches, barely, on that line.

**In Dargaard's Keep** *(a villanelle)*

In Dargaard's keep, where shadows fall,  
Loren Soth once dwelt, a noble knight,  
Duty his redemption and his downfall.

Saving the helpless from an ogre's maul,  
An elven beauty captivated his sight.  
In Dargaard's keep, where shadows fall.

But bound still to wedlock's sacred call,  
He could not yet taste of passion's bite.  
Duty his redemption and his downfall.

He had his wife and son slain in his hall,  
Then wed his beauty in a hollow rite.  
In Dargaard's keep, where shadows fall.

His Order condemned him, one and all;  
He fled his death and rode into the night.  
Duty his redemption and his downfall.

Offered redemption, he received a call  
From the gods, and he set out at first light.  
In Dargaard's keep, where shadows fall.

Told his bride was false, he rode home beneath a pall;  
His beauty he slew, and her curse his blight.  
Duty his redemption and his downfall.

Cursed to live, though ever death's thrall,  
Haunted for all done wrong and right.  
In Dargaard's keep, where shadows fall,  
Duty his redemption and his downfall.
