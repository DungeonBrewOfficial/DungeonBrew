# Prelude: The Paladin's Dream (Habbakuk)

*A solo prelude for Shadow of the Dragon Queen, loosely modeled on the module's "Broken Silence" prelude (a vision, a calling, a god revealing itself). The prose, the choice, and the rewards are mine.*

> **Structure.** One dream, one choice: give in to the pull of the water or resist it. Each branch carries its own reward.

> **GM note on the gods.** As in the cleric's vision, the god tells the character to keep its return hidden until "one in the south" brings word. The campaign is set before the gods of Krynn have openly returned, and the line reminds the player not to put miracles on public display. Replace **[name]** in the dialogue with the character's name, and adjust "my son" and other gendered references as needed.

---

## The Road

Toward the end of yet another day of travel, the sun's golden rays give way to twilight. A figure of noble bearing treks along with their retainers at their heels, eyes scanning for a suitable place to make camp. The journey has already been long, and there are still miles to go before reaching its end: the funeral of an old friend, one who set them not only on this path but on a longer one that would shape the long years still remaining to them.

*Ask the player to describe their character.*

In the center of a clearing, you find a small pond, its waters still and clear. As you gaze into it, the surface shimmers without any breeze, and for a fleeting moment you see a reflection that is not your own face but a majestic blue bird. The vision vanishes as quickly as it appeared. Before long you have set up camp for the evening and forgotten it completely.

## The Dream

Suddenly you are walking along the seashore, its tumultuous waves crashing, their spray dousing you. The sea roars. The sky roils, deep and black. The waves rise and fall, higher, then harder. You feel the pull of the sea, the call of the void, the desire to leap into the churning waters, but your mind recoils. It would mean death: crushed against the rocks, pulled out to the depths, breathless, helpless, and alone.

You wake. Your retainers sleep on, unaware. You are drenched in sweat, sitting beside your little pond, and you feel a similar tug at your mind.

**Do you succumb to the call, or resist?**

---

### Swim into the Pond

Whether through impulse or a draw from some otherworldly source, you throw yourself into the water. Immediately you feel yourself being pulled down. You try to swim back, kicking and pulling with your arms, but still you descend. The light of the surface dims, flickering away until only the crushing darkness of the deep greets you. Your lungs ache, straining for air. You cannot stop the descent. Your lips part. Water rushes in. Darkness takes you.

A voice in your head speaks:

> "Faith is the answer, even when you do not know what it will bring. I return you to life, [name], my son. Work still needs to be done. You may not know me, but I know you. I am Habbakuk, the Kingfisher. There is one in the south who brings word of my return, and until then you must keep my return hidden from all but a few whom you trust. Your faith must keep them true to their task."

You wake on the shore, sputtering water, your cough deep and ragged. Struggling to your feet, you look back at the water, still calm and clear, shimmering. You have no idea what possessed you to leap into it. Still, you feel... different, though you cannot say exactly how.

**Reward: Breath of Habbakuk.** You can use Lay on Hands on every non-hostile creature within 10 feet of you at once, at double the cost. Choose an amount of healing and spend twice that from your pool; each creature in range regains that amount. For example, healing everyone in range for 5 hit points costs 10 points.

---

### Don't Get in the Water

Days pass after leaving the pond. You still feel the sway of the waves, the need to delve into the depths, but you feel grounded. You resisted the temptation, resisted the urge, despite its pull. As you walk along contemplating these feelings, you notice a small blue bird flitting along beside you from branch to branch, twig to twig.

A voice in your head speaks:

> "You do not easily succumb to the call of darkness, [name], my son. You know your path, and you walk it well. You may not know me, but I know you. I am Habbakuk, the Kingfisher. There is one in the south who brings word of my return, and until then you must keep my return hidden from all but a few whom you trust. Your resolve must keep them true to their task. I grant you a symbol, a companion, so that you are never alone on your journey."

The small bird flits to your shoulder, chirping.

**Reward:** The character always has *find familiar* prepared. It doesn't count against their prepared spells and is cast using a paladin spell slot. The familiar can deliver the character's Lay on Hands.
