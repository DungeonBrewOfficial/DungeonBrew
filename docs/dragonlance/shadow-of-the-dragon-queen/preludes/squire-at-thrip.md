# Prelude: The Squire at Thrip

*A solo prelude for Shadow of the Dragon Queen, written for a squire of the Knights of Solamnia.*

> **Pronouns.** The text refers to the squire as "he." Adjust as needed.

> **Structure.** The squire stumbles into a dispute between three knights and a villager. Knowledge of the Measure and a read on the people involved shape a Persuasion check, and the squire chooses which side to back.

---

## Thrip

A small thorpe called Thrip, on the road to Maelgoth, is the first sign of civilization for days. It is a smattering of houses that sees few travelers. Most follow the rivers past it, and few watercraft put in unless they must. A weary, mud-stained traveler passes the first homes as the sun sinks low, looking for accommodations suitable to his station.

*Ask the player to describe their character.*

## The Dispute

Even the Knights of Solamnia keep no outpost in Thrip, which is why you are surprised to see three such knights in the distance. At first you feel a sense of relief that members of your order are here, and you think perhaps you can join them for a meal and a pint at whatever passes for an alehouse in Thrip. But their raised voices soon give you cause for concern.

You approach and see the largest of the three staring imperiously down his nose at an uncowed villager.

> *GM note:* The knights are led by Sir Garren Uth Durne, who stands back and lets the larger knight do the shouting.

> **Knight:** "The law is clear. Knights of Solamnia may secure camp on any necessary lands. You are responsible for your livestock, and they have damaged our goods and supplies. You are responsible for the costs incurred."

> **Joril:** "Stow it, ye feckin' twat. Even if I had the steel, which I do not, you set up on my land with no word. How am I supposed to know I can nay let me beasts graze on me own land? Show some common feckin' courtesy, ye honorless bobag."

> **Knight:** "And to call the honor of a Knight of Solamnia into question is a serious offense. I will see you brought before the High Council."

> **Joril:** "Feck my life. I have nary done nothin' wrong! All I'm doin' is tryin' to feed my feckin' family."

### Intelligence Check: The Measure

| DC | The character recalls |
|---|---|
| 5 | A person who knows of lawbreaking and does nothing to stop it is as responsible as the lawbreaker. Insubordination, failure to obey orders, masquerading as a knight, and cowardice are all offenses. |
| 10 | A leader never shouts, never loses their calm demeanor, never wavers or doubts, and never shows fear. Forgive those who wrong you. |
| 15 | Conduct that impinges on the honor of the Knighthood as a whole is a serious offense. Lies of omission violate the Measure. |

### Insight Check: Reading the Room

| DC | The character notices |
|---|---|
| 5 | Joril is trying to rile up the knights. |
| 10 | He truly cannot afford to cover the costs. |
| 15 | It is strange to have livestock grazing where the knights set up camp. |

### Get Involved?

Perhaps you don't have all the pieces of this conflict, but as an aspiring knight, you cannot simply walk away. The question is whether you try to convince the knights to let the matter go, or support their authority.

**DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion)** to calm the situation, modified by the Intelligence and Insight checks:

- **DC 5 results** are background information and don't change the roll.
- **DC 10 results** reduce the Persuasion DC by 1 for each check that reaches 10 (up to −2).
- **DC 15 results** each give the character an argument (see below). Making one argument to the knights grants advantage on the Persuasion check. Making both convinces Sir Garren to concede without a roll; he reins in the shouting knight himself.

**The pasture argument (Insight 15).** The beasts were grazing where the knights pitched camp, so the knights set up on Joril's working pasture. The law the knight cited lets them secure camp on *necessary* land. Thrip has empty ground, and they chose a farmer's grazing field.

**The omission argument (Intelligence 15).** Joril's real complaint is that the knights set up on his land with no word. Taking the land without telling its owner, then shouting him down for the result, is an omission that shames the order more than anything a farmer could say.

> *GM note:* The advantage comes from the player making the argument, not from passing the check. A player who succeeds on Insight but never raises the pasture gets nothing from it. Both arguments only support letting the matter go; a squire who backs the knights' authority uses the flat DC modifiers only.

### Failed Persuasion

The knight's patience runs out before anyone listens to you. Sir Garren has Joril bound for insulting the honor of the Knighthood and tells you, not unkindly, that a squire should learn when to hold his tongue. The knights break camp at dawn with Joril in tow, and his family watches them go.

*No reward. Sir Garren remembers the squire as the one who spoke out of turn.*

---

### Convince the Knights to Let the Matter Go

You mediate a fair resolution, suggesting a compromise that respects both parties' grievances, though not in quite such fanciful words. In the end, you offer a few coins from your own purse to help cover the costs and help the villager, Joril, round up his livestock. He is grateful for your help and admits he should not have egged the knights on so much. He gives you a leathern bracelet. You can tell it has little value, but he explains that it is one of the last pieces his father made before his passing, and he wants you to have it for your kindness.

**Reward:** **Joril's Leathern Bracelet**

### Support the Knights' Authority

You back your fellow knights. The Measure is clear, and Joril's beasts did the damage. You make him see it, though not gently. He has no coin to give, so he pays with what he has: the knights lead away his best ewe while his children watch from the doorway. Joril doesn't look at you again.

The knights, led by Sir Garren Uth Durne, are impressed by your command of the Measure, despite your being only a squire of the order. They invite you to join their camp, and you travel together until reaching Maelgoth, where they part ways to head for Heth. Before parting, Sir Garren gives you a stout warhorse, Karem, that belonged to a fallen companion.

**Reward:** **Karem** (warhorse)

## Homebrew Items

### Joril's Leathern Bracelet
*Treasure*  
**Cost** 5 sp

The bracelet is of little monetary value and there is nothing magical about it, but it serves as a reminder to defend the less fortunate. While wearing it, the wearer has advantage on saving throws against being frightened.
