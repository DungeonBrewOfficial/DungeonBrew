# Weapon Mastery

Weapon mastery is a measure of a character's skill with a specific type of weapon. The more skilled a character is with a type of weapon, the more likely they are to hit their foes and the more damage they inflict when they do hit. In addition, powerful and unique maneuvers are gained from advancing mastery with weapon types and styles, so that you have the ability to manipulate a battlefield more effectively.

For the rules governing mastery points, mastery levels, point progression, and mastery caps by class, see the [Mastery](../core/mastery-and-trials.md) chapter.

## Weapon Types

Weapons are divided into several categories based on their use in battle. Characters can focus their attention on learning these weapons by type to achieve different levels of mastery. Classes do not restrict which weapon types you can learn; your class's mastery ceiling and your attributes determine how well you use them.

* Axes
* Bludgeons
* Daggers
* Pole Weapons
* Projectiles
* Swords

<span id="short-stature"></span>**Short Stature.** Dwarves, gnomes, and halflings cannot wield Two-Handed or Heavy Projectile weapons. They can wield Versatile weapons with either grip.

### Non-Standard Attacks

<span id="unarmed"></span>**Unarmed.** Unarmed attacks use only the attacker's STR or PHY Tier Die. No weapon mastery die is added. Unarmed attacks are Fast and deal Crushing damage.

<span id="improvised"></span>**Improvised.** When a character attacks with an improvised weapon or a weapon they are Unskilled with, the GM assigns the weapon track that most closely matches the object's size and weight. The attack uses the Unskilled row of that track. Phase, attribute, and damage type follow the rules of the weapon category the object most resembles. Crushing is the default damage type when no clear analogue exists.

## Weapon Styles

In addition to being able to train with weapon types, you can also train in weapon styles. These styles allow you to benefit in different ways from the weapons you choose to wield. For instance, if you have trained in Weapon and Shield style, you benefit from having any one handed weapon in one hand and a shield in the other, even if you are not specifically trained with either.

There is a difference between knowing how to fight with a weapon and specializing in that style of fighting. Every character with weapon proficiency knows at least the basics of the fighting styles used with that weapon, but to gain additional benefits, must train.

<span id="single-weapon-style"></span>**Single Weapon Style**. The single weapon style requires that you wield only one Light One-Handed, Heavy One-Handed, or Versatile One-Handed in a single hand and nothing in your off-hand.

* You add your mastery level in this style (Unskilled 1 … Grand Master 7) to damage with single weapons.

Maneuvers gained from mastery in this style are only usable while wielding a weapon as described.

<span id="weapon-and-shield-style"></span>**Weapon and Shield Style**. The weapon and shield weapon style requires that you wield a Light One-Handed, Heavy One-Handed, or Versatile One-Handed weapon in your main hand and a shield in your off-hand.

* Wielding a shield in your off-hand allows you to take defense reactions against ranged attacks and can choose to allow your shield to absorb durability damage instead of your armor.
* You may attack with your shield for 1 AP. Shield attacks follow the I weapon dice track, deal Crushing damage, and use the same attribute as your main-hand weapon.

Maneuvers gained from mastery in this style are only usable while wielding a weapon with a shield as described.

<span id="two-handed-weapon-style"></span>**Two-Handed Weapon Style**. The two-handed weapon style requires that you wield only one weapon in two hands. The weapon can be Versatile Two-Handed or Two-Handed.

* When rolling weapon dice for a two-handed weapon attack, roll one additional die of the same type and drop the lowest result.

Maneuvers gained from mastery in this style are only usable while wielding a weapon as described.

<span id="two-weapon-style"></span>**Two-Weapon Style**. The two-weapon style requires that you wield a Light One-Handed, Heavy One-Handed, or Versatile One-Handed weapon in each hand.

* When you make an attack (1 AP), you attack with both your main-hand and off-hand weapons. Both weapons benefit from your attribute tier die.

Maneuvers gained from mastery in this style are only usable while wielding a weapon in each hand as described.

<span id="ranged-style"></span>**Ranged Style**. The ranged style requires that you wield a bow, crossbow, blowgun, sling, stave sling, or a weapon with the Thrown property.

* Ranged weapons require an Aiming Trial to hit (see [Combat](../core/combat.md) chapter).
* Ranged attacks can only be defended against if the defender is wielding a shield.
* Drawing or nocking ammunition, or drawing another thrown weapon of the same type, does not cost AP.
* Fixed projectiles (crossbows and blowguns) use fixed weapon dice as listed in their equipment description and do not benefit from weapon mastery dice. Their weapon dice are not affected by Dominance and Impotence; the Attack Attribute Die (Perception) is affected normally. The fixed projectile attack formula is: PER Tier Die + Fixed Weapon Dice + Modifiers.

When you reach Basic Mastery in the Ranged style, choose a doctrine: Marksman or Skirmisher. This choice is permanent. See Ranged [Style] Abilities below.

Maneuvers gained from mastery in this style are only usable while wielding a weapon as described.

## Using Maneuvers

You can only use one maneuver per action unless the maneuver specifically states otherwise. Each maneuver lists its AP cost, mastery requirement, trigger conditions, and effects.

<span id="frequency"></span>**Frequency**: You can use maneuvers as long as you have AP available but can only use one maneuver per action. Unless explicitly stated, a maneuver cannot be used as a part of the same Action or Reaction as another Skill Mastery Ability or Class Ability relying on the same type of action.

<span id="mastery-gating"></span>**Mastery Gating**: You cannot use maneuvers for a weapon unless you meet the minimum mastery requirement and are actively wielding the appropriate weapon.

<span id="borrowed-maneuvers"></span>**Borrowed Maneuvers**: When a weapon's properties name a maneuver from another weapon type (such as *[Hook](type-maneuvers.md#hook)* on a Khopesh), you can use that maneuver with that weapon. Use your mastery in the weapon's own type to meet the maneuver's mastery requirement.

<span id="style"></span>**Style**: Some maneuvers require specific weapon styles (single weapon, weapon and shield, two-handed). You can change styles during combat by drawing/stowing equipment.

### Maneuver Triggers

Every maneuver has a **trigger** that determines when it can be used. The trigger category appears first, followed by any specific conditions.

<span id="attack"></span>**Attack**: Used when you make a proactive attack on your turn. Most offensive maneuvers fall here. Declared before the attack roll unless stated otherwise.

<span id="counterattack"></span>**Counterattack**: Used after you successfully defend against an attack. Requires a successful defense and costs 1 AP.

<span id="reprisal"></span>**Reprisal**: Used when an enemy's movement or positioning creates an opening. Typically triggered when a creature enters or leaves your reach. Costs 1 AP.

<span id="defense"></span>**Defense**: Used when you are attacked. Declared after the attack is announced but before the roll.

<span id="declaration"></span>**Declaration**: Committed at the start of the round during the Declaration Phase. These maneuvers lock you into a specific posture or behavior for the entire round.

<span id="contingency"></span>**Contingency**: Triggered when a specific condition is met during combat. The condition follows the keyword and can only be used when the situation arises.

## Melee Guards

Guards represent how a fighter presents their weapon and prepares for combat. A character declares one guard (or formation, if trained in Tactics) at the start of each round. This declaration is locked for the round unless an ability states otherwise. Guards are unlocked automatically as your highest melee weapon type mastery advances, the same as Melee Baseline Maneuvers.

Guards are not stances in the defensive sense — they are checkpoints in offensive motion that prepare attacks and shape the tactical options available to you.

### Untrained Guards

Available at Untrained mastery. These three guards offer simple trade-offs between offense and defense.

<span id="mutable"></span>**Mutable** *(Fiore's Instabile)*
No modifications to attack or defense rolls. The neutral guard.

<span id="stable"></span>**Stable** *(Fiore's Stabile)*
Your defense rolls have Dominance. Your attack rolls have Impotence.

<span id="striking"></span>**Striking** *(Fiore's Pulsativa)*
Your attack rolls have Dominance. Your defense rolls have Impotence.
