# Mortal Folk

Mortal Folk are mortals who think and speak: the bandit on the road, the soldier at the gate, the hunter in the wood, the knight in a lord's service. Their bodies are flesh and nothing more, and they share no nature that sets them apart from the world.

They want what people want: coin, safety, a meal, orders followed, a grudge settled. Many fights with Mortal Folk can be talked down, paid off or outlasted, and the ones that can't usually began long before the party arrived.

<span id="classification-package"></span>**Classification package:** Mortal Folk have no innate immunity or vulnerability. Connected to Void magic.

<span id="kingdoms"></span>**Kingdoms.** Folk are one of the two mortal kingdoms. Folk think and speak, and are connected to Void. Beasts do not, and are connected to Primal.

## Compact, Military

A military compact is a body of soldiers bound by contract: sanctioned (a crown's or city's paid army), unsanctioned (a free company, deserters, rebels), or sellswords hired by whoever can pay. A company fights as a unit. The lieutenant forms the line, the standard-bearer holds its center where every soldier can see the banner, and the captain fights wherever the line is weakest. Soldiers fight for pay and for each other: take the banner and the company wavers, and kill the captain and it becomes a mob fighting for its life or its loot. A party meets a company on the march, holding a crossing for its employer, or between contracts and hungry.

## Compact, Outlaw

An outlaw compact is a band bound by shared purpose outside the law: highwaymen, poachers' gangs, smugglers. Their contract is a share of the take and each other's silence. A band strikes where it holds the advantage, with an ambush on a lonely road or a toll at a ford, and it runs from a fair fight. What the band wants decides whether a victim who hands over the purse walks away. The leader holds the band together through fear or profit, and when the leader falls, the band scatters. A party meets outlaws on the road, as the reason a village is afraid, or as a bounty. A band that grows large enough draws the attention of a lord or a crown, and either breaks or becomes something else.

## Tradition, Priscan

Priscan casters hold the power of the numen in something they carry: spells bound into objects, sunk into the bloodline, written in a compendium, or inscribed on the skin. Whatever holds a caster's spells is something the party might find, steal, bargain for, or destroy, and something the caster will go a long way to keep or recover. A Priscan caster could serve a faith, an order, a patron, or no one, as the GM sees fit.

## Vassalage

A vassalage is a lord's household, bound to them by oath. At a minor lord's holding, the militia are the levy, called up from the fields when the lord needs them. The guards keep the lord's gate and roads, the foresters walk the lord's woods and keep the game law, the sergeant drills the soldiers, and the household knight rides at the lord's side. The household fights for its lord and breaks when the lord falls: the militia go home, the guards look to their own, and the knight, with no one left to hold their oath, picks their own fight. A party meets a vassalage at its lord's gate, on the roads, poaching in the woods, or at war with the lord.

The same household repeats at every scale, each member standing higher with the lord's station:

<span id="vassal-stations-by-tier"></span>**Vassal Stations by Tier**

| Tier | Station | Its lord |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | A knight's manor: a hamlet or village | a landed knight |
| 2 | A minor lordship: several manors | a minor lord, such as a baron or thane |
| 3 | A great lordship: a county or earldom | a great lord, such as an earl or count |
| 4 | A duchy or march | a magnate, such as a duke or margrave |
| 5 | A principality or petty kingdom | a prince or petty king or queen |
| 6 | A kingdom | a king or queen |
| 7 | An empire | an emperor or empress |
