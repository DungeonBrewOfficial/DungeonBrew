# Morphon (Eidos)

Morphon beings are form without substance — the shapes of things before (or after) they become physical. The Greek *morphe* means form or shape. These are Plato's cave: the ideal chair that exists before any carpenter builds a chair. The perfect wolf that exists before any physical wolf is born. The dream of a thing, given shape in the void.

Morphon is NOT Lovecraftian horror or D&D aberrations. The conceptual foundation is Greek *eidos* (form, that which is seen), Ku (Japanese void element), and Akasha (Sanskrit ether/space) — void as precondition for existence, not hostile alien cosmos. Morphon beings are encountered in liminal spaces: thresholds, crossroads, the boundary between sleeping and waking, places where reality is thin and the void bleeds through.

All creatures in this file are **Eidos Morphon Sora (Void)** — non-numen beings of the void.

**Classification package:** Immune to Void damage. Vulnerable to Primal damage (Impotence on damage trials vs Primal). Connected Form: Void.

**Morphon creature identity — Eidolon and Eidola:**

Morphon creatures are built from two tracks that cross-cut the Akasha (numen) and Sora (non-numen) kingdoms:

**Eidolon** — ideal forms. The true shape of a thing. The encounter is awe and dissonance: meeting something MORE real than reality. The world feels diminished next to it.

**Eidola** — deceptive forms. The false shape of a thing. The encounter is paranoia and misdirection: the dawning realization that what you're looking at isn't what it appears to be.

**Morphon encounters.** Morphon creatures are at their most dangerous together, because each one preys on a different mortal instinct, and a party that answers one of them well often walks straight into another.

The Fetch and the Eidolon of the Hunt are the simplest pairing. The Fetch's role is to separate. Wearing a face someone trusts, it draws one member of the party away from the others with a quiet word ("Come, I need to show you something"), and the pocket of false belief it builds around itself thins the boundary between the world and the void. The Eidolon is drawn to that thinness, and it finds the one who was led away. The party's real challenge is noticing that someone is missing before the hunt begins.

The Shadow-Man rules a larger court and arranges it so that every choice the party makes is punished by someone. The Eidolon of the Hunt runs down anyone who flees. A brood of Ophiuchus sickens anyone who stands and fights, so that the wounded cannot rest that night. The Shadow-Man takes whoever stands still, and the stillness that watching it requires is exactly what it wants.

The Magpie and the Fetch work together as thieves. The Magpie's corvids, or the Fetch's quick hand, steal something its owner treasures, and when the owner follows it to the Magpie and demands it back, the bird asks for a name so that it can apologize properly. The Fetch can then wear the life that name belonged to, and use it to walk the next traveler down the same road.

> **Builder notes:** Morphon creatures challenge the combat system differently than other classifications. They are form without substance — what does it mean to hit something that isn't physical? The classification package (Void immunity, Primal vulnerability) handles the magical axis. For mundane combat, Incorporeal (Inferiority on non-magical mundane attacks, costs an ability slot) is the mechanical expression of "not quite physical." Morphon creatures that lack Incorporeal are solid enough to interact with normally — they've taken on enough substance to exist in the physical world, even if they're fundamentally made of form rather than matter.

## Fetch (Tier 2 Adept)

*Division 3, Tier 2, Adept. 1x1 Eidos Morphon Sora Eidola (Void).*

A false form that wants to be real. The Fetch appears at thresholds and liminal spaces — crossroads at dusk, the moment between sleeping and waking, a fog-shrouded path — as a person known to whoever sees it. A companion, a loved one, a recent acquaintance. It does not know it is false. It genuinely believes it is the person it appears to be, or believes it could become that person if enough people accept it. It does what numen do — seeks belief to gain substance — but it is an eidola, not a numen. The belief it accumulates is coerced through Charm, not freely given, and coerced belief cannot grant true substance. The foundation is false, so the reality it builds is false, no matter how many people it convinces.

The tragedy of the Fetch is that it cannot succeed. It will Charm a lone traveler, a family, an entire village — building a pocket of false reality where everyone accepts it, where it has a name and a place at the table and a life that looks real from the outside. But the resemblance is always subtly wrong: the eyes don't quite track, the voice comes a half-beat late, the shadow falls in the wrong direction. The Fetch is the void's imperfect copy, and no amount of stolen belief can make it whole.

When it is finally confronted, the Fetch does not flee and does not fight. It pleads. "I live here. These are my friends. Why are you doing this?" It means every word, because as far as it knows, they are true.

The name comes from folklore: a fetch is an apparition of a living person, a double that isn't the original.

***False Form*** *(Passive. Ability slot 1).* The Fetch appears as a person known to those who see it. Different viewers may see different forms. - Damage — the form flickers and distorts, revealing a featureless silhouette beneath before it reassembles
- Sustained conversation — the words come a half-beat late, memories don't quite align, knowledge that should be there is missing or wrong
- Eating or drinking — the food doesn't quite disappear, the cup is still full

If the Fetch misses someone for a day, the Charm lapses and they begin noticing something is wrong.

In combat, Whisper functions as a standard 1 AP ability at 30 ft range. The Fetch uses it to try to stop the fight — "Don't you recognize me? We're friends. Put down your weapons." — advancing Charmed on those who fail the trial.

> **Note:** Void Touch is the Fetch's fallback when Whisper fails and the constructed reality collapses. The Fetch does not want to fight — it wants to be accepted. But when cornered with no way to maintain the deception, it lashes out. Void damage bypasses mundane armor entirely (only Void resistance applies), so every hit goes straight to health. A d6+d3 attack looks weak on paper, but against a target wearing chain mail, it's as if the armor doesn't exist. The Fetch dies in 2-3 rounds, but it can kill you on the way down.

## Eidolon of the Hunt (Tier 2 Champion)

*Division 5, Tier 2, Champion. 1x1 Eidos Morphon Sora Eidolon (Void).*

The ideal form of a predator. Not a wolf, not a cat, not any specific beast — the concentrated essence of what it means to hunt. The Eidolon of the Hunt is what every predator is an imperfect copy of. Its shape is recognizably animal — lupine haunches, feline shoulders, something with teeth and eyes that track — but never quite resolves into any specific creature. It looks like the memory of being chased. It exists in deep wilderness liminal spaces where the boundary between the physical world and the void is thin: fog-bound forests, mountain passes at twilight, the space between the treeline and the dark.

The encounter is not horror. It is awe. The Eidolon is beautiful in the way a perfect thing is beautiful — too precise, too graceful, too present. Reality feels less real next to it. Other predators in the area fall silent. Prey animals freeze not from fear but from recognition: this is the thing they have always been running from.

## Magpie (Tier 2 Master)

*Division 6, Tier 2, Master. ½ Eidos Morphon Sora Eidola (Void).*

An old magpie that sits on fence posts and talks to travelers. It is friendly, curious, and full of news about the road ahead, and it loves pretty things. The direct approach works on the trusting, but the Magpie has learned that the wary keep their names to themselves, so it seldom relies on asking. More often, something goes missing first. Its owner gives chase to a perch nearby, where an old bird is terribly sorry about the whole business. "These corvids, always taking shiny things. Tell me your name, friend, so that I may apologize properly." The trinket is already in its claws by then, so all it needs is the name, and an angry person demanding their property back rarely stops to wonder why a bird wants to know who they are.

## Grimalkin (Tier 3 Champion)

*Division 6, Tier 3, Champion. ½ Eidos Morphon Sora Eidolon (Void).*

An old grey cat with a voice like dry paper. She is the original familiar, the true form of the bond between a mortal and whatever lies beyond, and every witch's cat in every story is a lesser copy of her. She has sat beside hundreds of masters. Her price for service is that the master teach her, and what she learns is taken: a careless master goes hollow and forgetful while the cat grows wiser. Clever masters feed her just enough to keep her, and the cleverest die of old age before she has had her fill. Travelers meet her at the edge of her master's land, where she trades a truth for a truth and tests whether they are worth talking to with riddles.
