# Fae

Fae creatures are sustained by Primal magic — the magic of growth, decay, and the cyclical rhythms of nature. They do not age biologically; their relationship to time is governed by emotional or spiritual resonance. Grief, strife, and pain age them; peace and equilibrium preserve them.

All creatures in this file are **Fae (Primal)** unless otherwise noted.

**Builder notes:** Fae are diverse — elves, orcs, goblins, dryads, and spirits are all equally Fae. The common thread is Primal magic. Casters need Primal Form to charm, bind, summon, or banish these creatures. Mind magic does not work for non-damage interaction (unlike Mortal creatures).

## Fae Goblins — Tuatha

Goblins in Mastery are not the disposable D&D green-skins. They are Fae beings — spiritual, mischievous, sometimes malevolent, sometimes helpful. They are tied to the Primal world and often possess minor supernatural abilities (invisibility, shapeshifting, illusion). Hobgoblins, brownies, boggarts, and other fairy-tale goblins are all subtypes within this category.

## Orcs — Fae Tuatha

Orcs are Fae. Their violence is not corruption — it is simply what Primal nature looks like at this tempo. They age through grief like all Fae, but their violent nature generates grief faster than they can process it. Their response to pain is violence, which creates more pain, which demands more violence.

## Sídhe — Place-Bound Fae Spirits

Sídhe are Fae spirits tied to a specific place or living thing. A dryad IS her tree. A river spirit IS the river. Remove them from their place and they weaken, wither, and eventually die. Within their place, they are formidable — the terrain itself responds to them.
