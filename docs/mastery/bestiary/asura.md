# Asura

Asura are sustained by Sacred magic: life, purity and restoration. Angels, celestial guardians and holy beasts are all Asura. Mortal faith shapes them: what enough people believe is holy, and tell stories about as holy, becomes Sacred.

Asura want what Sacred magic is: things kept pure, preserved and restored. Mortals call that good; the Asura do not think in those terms. A Cherub will burn a starving thief at the threshold it guards as readily as a murderer, and the purity an Asura serves does not bend to mortal need. A party meets them guarding what they were set to guard, purging what they judge corrupted, and pursuing purposes that rarely ask what mortals want.

<span id="classification-package"></span>**Classification package (all Asura):** Immune to Sacred damage. Impotence on trials vs Profane damage. Connected to Sacred magic.

<span id="kingdoms"></span>**Kingdoms.** Asura belong to one of two kingdoms. **Amesha Spenta** are Asura numen, powerful enough to grant magic to those who devote themselves to them: numen that worship placed on the Sacred side of its faith. **Yazata** are all other Asura: beings that serve the Amesha Spenta or come from them, and creatures that holy stories have made Sacred.

### Four-Eyed Dog (Tier 2 Champion)

*Division 5, Tier 2, Champion. 1×1 Asura Yazata (Sacred).*

*Yellow Dog*

A yellow dog with a spot above each eye, like a second pair of eyes. It belongs to the one who meets the dead at the crossing, and where her bridge dogs stay to hold it, this one she sends out: to wherever the dead are not resting, or something has taken one of them. Where the stories made the churchyard's first dog a thing of the grave, it became the Grim. Where they made it holy, it became this.

<span id="what-the-stories-say_3"></span>**What the stories say.** Asura Lore can turn up these stories. Which of them are true is the GM's choice.

- <span id="success_3"></span>**Success:** It is a Four-Eyed Dog, an Asura, sent out by the one who meets the dead at the crossing. Daeva flee from its look.
- <span id="success-by-2_3"></span>**Success by 2:** One story, which the GM chooses and may make different for each PC:
    - *It goes only where the dead are not resting, so wherever one is seen, something nearby has died badly or refused to stay dead.*
    - *Its look can drive a Daeva out of a body it has taken.*
    - *It brings the lost dead back to the crossing, and the living who follow it may find the crossing too.*
- <span id="success-by-4_3"></span>**Success by 4:** All three.

<span id="for-the-gm_2"></span>**For the GM:** Whether its look can drive a Daeva out of a host is the GM's choice. It never frees a Cursed: that bond can't be broken from outside.

<span id="glance"></span>***Glance*** *(2 AP, Action. Ability slot 3).* The dog looks at one undead creature or Daeva within 30 feet that it can see. The creature must succeed on a CHA trial [6]. <span id="behavior_5"></span>**Behavior:** The dog is always on an errand for its mistress. It ignores the living unless they get in its way, goes straight for the restless dead and whatever keeps them from resting, and turns Daeva away from the dead it was sent for. When the errand is done, it goes.

### Vohunazga (Tier 3 Epic)

*Division 8, Tier 3, Epic. 1×1 Asura Yazata (Sacred).*

*The Stray*

A dog with no master and no marks, which looks like any stray on any road. It serves no one, not even the one who meets the dead at the crossing. It goes where it likes and lives on what it is given. It is holy all the same, and it hunts what is unclean: vermin, serpents, the restless dead, and whatever keeps the dead or a Daeva close. People say it has something of every kind of person in it: priest and warrior, thief and child.

<span id="what-the-stories-say_4"></span>**What the stories say.** Asura Lore can turn up these stories. Which of them are true is the GM's choice.

- <span id="success_4"></span>**Success:** It is a Vohunazga, an Asura: a dog with no master, held holy. It hunts the unclean, and whoever strikes it is cursed.
- <span id="success-by-2_4"></span>**Success by 2:** One story, which the GM chooses and may make different for each PC:
    - *It comes to a door as a stray, and how a house feeds it is how the house is judged.*
    - *Whoever harms it finds no help at the crossing when they die: the dogs that keep the crossing will not come to them.*
    - *Where it has hunted, nothing unclean is left: no vermin in the walls, and no dead walking.*
- <span id="success-by-4_4"></span>**Success by 4:** All three.

<span id="for-the-gm_3"></span>**For the GM:** What feeding or harming a Vohunazga means beyond the encounter is the GM's choice, including what waits at the crossing for someone it cursed.

<span id="abilities_5"></span>**Abilities:**

<span id="snatch"></span>***Snatch*** *(1 AP, Attack [Fast]. Ability slot 3).* Targets a creature holding something in its hand: a weapon, a focus, or another item. On a hit with a Bite attack, instead of dealing damage, the target must succeed on a STR or PHY trial [9] (its choice). On a failure, the dog takes the item in its jaws and can move with it. It drops the item in its space when it makes a Bite attack or takes damage, and it can drop it at any time.

<span id="run-down"></span>***Run Down*** *(2 AP, Movement + Attack [Fast]. Ability slot 4).* The dog moves up to its speed toward one creature it can see or sense; this movement does not provoke Reprisal Attacks. It then makes a Bite attack against that creature. Against a Daeva, an undead creature, or a creature Connected to Profane magic, the attack has Dominance.

<span id="phase-reference_1"></span>**Phase Reference:** All abilities use Bite (Fast) and are available in both active phases.

<span id="behavior_6"></span>**Behavior:** The dog wanders, comes to doors and camps, and takes what it is given. It ignores anyone who leaves it alone and has nothing unclean about them. It comes for Daeva, the undead, and anyone who keeps the dead or a Daeva at their side, and for anyone who has struck it or turned it away hungry. It doesn't stand and trade blows: it darts in, takes what it can, bites, and is gone before anyone can close on it. It never leaves a hunt unfinished.
