## Textiles and Clothing

Clothing not only protects you from the elements but also serves as a status symbol. Silks, fine wools, and embroidery are expected from higher classes and a lack of these finer elements is a sign that someone is of a lower class. First impressions can go a long way in determining how PCs and NPCs are treated in social situations. Worn clothing is non-encumbering. Carried clothing counts as one Light object for every 5 pieces, unless the table lists its own encumbrance. Garments without a description are what their names suggest.

**Broadcloth, Fine**. Dense plain wool, spun into a fine, thinner cloth usually with an ornate weave, such as twill.

**Broadcloth, Plain**. Dense plain wool spun into a plain, coarse cloth for general use usually with a plain weave.

**Broadcloth, Scarlet**. The finest wool broadcloth, heavily fulled and shorn until dense and smooth, then dyed with kermes, the costliest dye of all. Scarlet names the cloth rather than its color, and a scarlet can be red, blue, black, or green.

**Canvas**. Hemp spun cloth, usually in a plain weave. Used for utilitarian objects like tents and bags.

**Cloth of Gold**. Silk woven with gold thread: a narrow strip of silver, gilded on its surface and wound around a silk or linen core. The gold thread usually forms the weft, over a warp of fine yellow silk or wool. The price varies with the fineness of the silk, the weave, and how much metal it carries.

**Cloth of Gold, Pure**. Cloth of gold woven with strips of nearly pure gold rather than gilded silver. The older way of making it, and the heaviest and costliest of the gold cloths.

**Cloth of Gold, Raised**. Cloth of gold woven with an extra gold weft, raised in loops so that the gold stands up from the surface. It carries far more metal than plain cloth of gold, and takes far longer to weave.

**Cloth of Silver**. Fabric woven with threads of silver. The threads can be linen or silk that are then wrapped in a narrow strip of almost pure silver. Linen produces a thicker thread and silk produces a finer thread, being more expensive. These threads are woven into a fabric, usually comprising the weft, oftentimes the warp being fine white wool or silk. The price can vary depending on the quality of the materials used, the type of weave, and the purity of the silver.

**Linen, Fine**. Linen spun from flax into a softer, thinner cloth that might have an ornate weave. Dyes might be applied for an additional cost, but it can also be its natural color.

**Linen, Plain**. Hempen linen is generally more coarse with a broader weave. Low-cost dyes might be applied for an additional cost, but it is often its natural color.

**Silk, Fine**. Fine silk almost always includes ornate weaves, and can be brocaded, embroidered, and usually dyed rich colors.

**Silk, Plain**. Plain silk is usually defined by the plainness of the weave and the lack of patterning.

**Tabard**. A tabard is a piece of cloth worn over armor to help keep rain, mud, blood, and other grime off in order to keep the armor from rusting. Knights also wear tabards to display their colors or sigil.

**Velvet, Fine**. Fabric with a pile made of silk thread. Fine velvet is made with complex weaves or patterns, is often dyed with rich colors, and is always backed with silk.

**Velvet, Plain**. Fabric with a pile made of silk thread. Plain velvet is made with simple weaves, is unadorned, and may be backed with cheaper fabrics, such as wool.

**Wool, Fine**. Fabric woven of finer fibers of wool that creates a softer, "hairier" cloth. Less durable, but a much finer fabric with a less noticeable weave.

**Wool, Thick**. Fabric woven of thicker fibers of wool that creates a sturdy cloth. The weave is generally more visible than finer types of wool.

Roll 2d[Physique Tier Die] to set the entanglement DC. The target makes a Strength Trial against your DC. On failure, the target advances [Restricted](../core/conditions.md#restricted "Strength trial to resist. Grappling, binding, entanglement, or physical constraint. Tiers compound. A Restricted creature can spend 1 AP to cut the bola with a bladed weapon or make a Strength Trial against the original DC to pull free. Range: 20/40 (second increment with Impotence).

**Block and Tackle**. Wooden pulley system intended for use with Hemp Rope. For the purposes of lifting or pulling objects, force is doubled for each additional pulley.

**Caltrops**. A caltrop is a four-pronged iron spike crafted so that one prong faces up no matter how the caltrop comes to rest. You scatter caltrops on the ground in the hope that your enemies step on them or are at least forced to slow down to avoid them. One 4-pound bag of caltrops covers an area 5 feet square.

Any creature that enters the area must succeed on an Agility trial (DC 4) or stop moving this turn and take 1 Piercing damage. Taking this damage reduces the creature's movement by 10 feet for 1 exploration round. A creature moving through the area at half speed doesn't need to roll the trial.

**Candle, Beeswax**. Candles made from beeswax, which are usually associated with a pleasant smell. Lasts about 2 hours and provides 10 feet of light and 10 feet of ambient light. Can be placed in a lantern to avoid snuffing it out due to wind or rain.

**Candle, Tallow**. Candles made from animal fat, which have a distinctly unpleasant smell that sometimes attracts beasts or predatory creatures. Lasts about 2 hours and provides 10 feet of light and 10 feet of ambient light. Can be placed in a lantern to avoid snuffing it out due to wind or rain.

**Codex**. A bound book used by practitioners of magic and alchemy to record spells, prayers, recipes, or observations. It is not uncommon for metalwork to protect the corners, spine, or add definition to the cover. Additional vellum can be bound in by a skilled bookbinder, or the practitioner can start a second volume.

## Lodging and Services

Travelers often find the need to pay for services when on the road. Sometimes, these services are as simple as lodging for the night, but could also include laundry and bathing to wash the grime of the road off. Clothing could need mending, armor repairing, weapons need fixing, and any number of similar services.

**Bath**. Either part of a public bath house or provided within an inn. Water is heated and brought to the vessel along with soap and towels. Places that provide baths usually provide laundry service as well for an additional fee. People who do not bathe regularly (once per week) have Impotence on Presence trials until they do.

**Bearer/Porter**. These individuals are laborers who will carry whatever is directed. Each is able to carry up to 50 pounds individually, or double that with a carrying pole or litter.

**Blacksmith**. This occupation assumes most metalworking jobs. A blacksmith might be hired to repair armor, craft a weapon, or provide other metalwork. The cost per day does not include materials that might be needed for the work.

**Carpenter**. This occupation assumes most woodworking jobs. A carpenter might be hired to secure a portal, fashion a chest, etc. The cost per day does not include materials that might be needed for the work.

**City Rooms (Poor)**. Rent a small cottage, which can sleep 6. In larger cities, the cottage is likely in a poorer part of town.

**City Rooms (Common)**. Rent a craftsman's house, which can sleep 8 and may also contain a crafting space, such as a smithy, cordwainer, apothecary, or something similar.

**City Rooms (Good)**. Rent a well-furnished merchant's house, which can sleep 10 and may also contain a crafting space, such as a smithy, cordwainer, apothecary, or something similar. In addition, these homes sometimes have an additional residence attached.

**Guide**. A guide will show travelers around the city for a day, pointing out reputable merchants, taverns, and possibly sights of interest. Oftentimes, they make their living on providing instructions for copper farthings.

**Healer**. A healer treats one injury, trauma, or condition. Healer will also charge for the cost of medicinal supplies. Cost is per tier of supplies used.

**Inn Lodging (Poor)**. Open room with a cot or hay on the floor that does not include meals.

**Inn Lodging (Common)**. Closed room shared with multiple occupants, which may or may not require sharing beds and does not include meals.

**Inn Lodging (Good)**. Closed room with a single bed, chest, and washbasin that does not include meals.

**Kenneling Dog**. For feed and kenneling of dogs. Rarely, other animals might be accepted for an additional fee.

**Laundry**. A load of laundry usually consists of a single outfit. Additional charges should be applied for cleaning/polishing boots, cleaning armor, and similar effects. People who do not launder regularly (once per week) have Impotence on Presence trials until their clothes are cleaned.

**Leather Worker**. This occupation is principally concerned with the fabrication of leather goods such as back packs, belts, straps, horse tack, etc. The cost per day does not include materials that might be needed for the work.

**Limner**. These individuals do all sign painting, drawing of heraldic devices, etc. The ability to write, draw, and perform heraldic services is rare and expensive. Extremely skilled individuals demand up to 1 gs for a day's work.

**Linkboy**. A linkboy is a torch or lantern bearer. They are often youngsters, but grown men also serve.

**Mason**. Any stonework must be done by a mason, and this occupation subsumes plasterers as well. The cost per day does not include materials that might be needed for the work.

**Meal (Poor)**. Simple food that might include barley bread, pea pottage, rabbit or squirrel, and cup of ale.

**Meal (Common)**. Common food, that might include bread, bean pottage, fish, and a cup of beer.

**Meal (Banquet)**. Luxurious food, that might include white bread, venison or boar, spiced vegetables, figs and dates, and wine.

**Minstrel**. Minstrels usually are hired to perform at certain locations, but may also perform on street corners or in taverns for tips. In general, they will take requests for pay.

**Mourner**. Mourners can be hired to attend a funeral, usually to increase attendance for important individuals who have died.

**Pack Handler**. These individuals are trained at loading, handling, and unloading beasts of burden, carts, or wagons.

**Riding Horse, Rent**. Rent a riding horse for the day. It is expected that the horse is fed, watered, and brushed down if necessary. If the horse is lost, stolen, or maimed, the renter is expected to reimburse the owner for the value of the horse.

**Scribe**. A scribe will write a single document, or 400 words. This cost does not include materials, which can increase the cost. The scribe may also be hired to include additional artistry for a similar cost.

**Sedan Chair, Hired**. Sedan chairs can sometimes be rented to taxi an occupant around the city. They generally charge by the hour, a minimum of 1 st. The same amount is charged for waiting, and charged again if the travel would take the chair beyond the city walls.

**Stabling Animals**. For feed and stabling of small or medium horses, mules, or donkeys.

**Stabling Large Animals**. For feed and stabling of oxen, draft horses, or war horses.

**Tailor**. This occupation makes and repairs clothing, bags, shield covers, etc. It also subsumes hatters. Tailors skilled in crafting with silk, velvet, and other fine materials command up to 1 sc per day. The cost per day does not include materials that might be needed for the work.

**Teamster**. Teamsters drive carts and wagons. They will also load and unload their vehicles. They are expert animal handlers with respect to their particular specialty of draft animal only. They have their own wagon or cart in addition to their own animal.

**Transport 1 Tun (Land)**. Cost to transport 1 tun, (40 ft.<sup>3</sup>, 256 gallons, or 2,100 lbs.), each mile over land.

**Transport 1 Tun (Water)**. Cost to transport 1 tun, (40 ft.<sup>3</sup>, 256 gallons, or 2,100 lbs.), each mile over water.

**Travel, Sea**. The cheapest means of securing passage on a ship usually entails either being cooped up in the cargo hold or on the deck in the elements for the duration. Generally speaking, the quality of a given traveler's accommodations are about half a day's wages for a given social class, with the most expensive being a private cabin at about 1 gs per day.

**Valet/Lackey**. This occupation subsumes the various forms of body servants and messengers.
