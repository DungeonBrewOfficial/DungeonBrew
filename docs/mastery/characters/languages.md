# Languages

Every character speaks several tongues. You speak Koine, the trade tongue shared across the lands; the tongue of the culture that raised you; and the tongue of your class. During play, you may learn others through your skills, your factions, or time spent among those who speak them.

## Kinds of Tongue

### Vulgaris

Vulgaris is the everyday tongue of a culture, the speech of its homes, markets, and fields. Each culture has its own Vulgaris, and most have dialects that mark where a speaker is from. A nation that holds several cultures holds more than one Vulgaris.

### Aulicus

Aulicus is the court register of a Vulgaris: its pronunciation, its manners, and its careful, indirect turns of phrase. Anyone who speaks a Vulgaris understands its Aulicus. Speaking it is what lets you pass at court. A speaker without it is understood, but heard as a commoner, a provincial, or an outsider. A nobility that speaks several tongues may have an Aulicus for each.

### High Tongues

Dwarves, elves, gnomes, halflings, and orcs each have a high tongue, shared by every member of their kind across all of their nations. Each of their cultures also has its own Vulgaris, descended from the high tongue, which a speaker from another nation may not follow. When two dwarves of different nations meet, they speak Ena, because it is the one tongue they are certain to share.

| Species | High Tongue |
|:---|:---|
| Dwarf | Ena |
| Gnome | Glōssa |
| Gray Elf | Kieli |
| Halfling | Tunge |
| Orc | Mihi |
| Sylvan Elf | Iaith |

Dwarves guard Ena closely and rarely speak it in front of others.

### Koine

Koine is the trade tongue, spoken by merchants, travelers, soldiers, and anyone whose life crosses borders. Every character speaks Koine. Because it is shared so widely, Koine is also the base tongue that jargons ride on when their speakers come from different cultures.

### Jargons

A jargon is a way of speaking a tongue that hides its meaning from outsiders. It swaps common words for its own, bends familiar words to new meanings, and carries its own cadence and pronunciation. A jargon needs a base tongue beneath it, so two speakers of a jargon must also share a Vulgaris or Koine. To an outsider, a jargon sounds like ordinary speech, but its meaning slips past them.

Rotwelsch, commonly known as thieves' cant, is the speech of thieves, bandits, and the criminal underworld.

Castrensis is the speech of the camp, picked up by anyone who has trained or served as a soldier.

### Argot

Most factions keep an argot: private words, phrases, passwords, and signs known only to their members. An argot marks you as one of a particular faction, as a jargon marks you as one of a trade. Each faction's argot is its own, and a rival's argot is as foreign as any other tongue.

### Tradition Tongues

Each magical tradition keeps a learned tongue for its magic. A tradition tongue is always known in full: whoever learns it learns all of it.

Bérla na filed is the tongue of the Telluric tradition, spoken by Druids and Filis. It lives in meter and cadence as much as in words, and it is never written. It can only be learned from a living speaker willing to teach it.

Magian is the tongue of the Occult tradition, spoken by the Bahador and Magus. Each circle of magi keeps its own argot within it.

The Theurgic tradition has three tongues, one for each entropic form: Manthra for the Sacred, Nefas for the Profane, and Apophatic for the Void.

Rún is the tongue of the Priscan tradition, spoken by the Cursed, Gnomes, Gray Elves, and Sylvan Elves. Its practitioners write it in runes, and Sylvan Elves carry those runes on their skin. Scholars of other traditions call it Prisca.

### Dead Tongues

Tongues no longer spoken survive in old writing and strange symbols. Learning them is part of Ancient Lore (see *[Skill Mastery](../skills/index.md)*).

## Learning a Tongue

Each route below teaches a tongue. A route teaches nothing new if you already speak its tongue.

### Factions

A faction can teach two kinds of tongue: the tongue of its trade, shared with everyone in that line of work, and its own argot.

| Faction | Tongue of Its Trade | Argot |
|:---|:---|:---|

Whether a kindred takes in an outsider, and teaches them its tongue, is for the GM to decide.

### Living Among Speakers

You can learn a Vulgaris or Koine by living among those who speak it. The GM determines how long that takes, based on how immersed you are and how hard you try.

## Speaking and Understanding

Speakers who share no tongue can follow each other only through gestures and goodwill.

Speakers of a jargon can talk openly in front of outsiders. To an outsider, a jargon is a spoken code, deciphered as in Cryptography (see *[Skill Mastery](../skills/index.md)*, [Thievery](../skills/thievery.md)). Catching a jargon's meaning as it is spoken is hard. The GM may set a higher DC to decipher it in the moment, and a lower DC to work it out later, when you have time to turn the words over.

How you speak marks where you come from. A commoner at court is understood, but heard as a commoner, and a traveler is known by their dialect. The GM decides how a listener receives what they hear.

## Optional Rules

### Regional Koine

With this rule, Koine is no longer universal. The GM determines which characters speak it, based on their upbringing and travels.

### Passing and Fluent

With this rule, a Vulgaris or Koine can be known at two levels. A passing speaker gets by: prices, directions, and simple talk. A fluent speaker follows everything, including jokes, insults, and double meanings. Tongues from your culture, class, skills, and factions are fluent. Tongues learned by living among speakers begin as passing, and the GM determines whether your Koine is passing or fluent.

While you speak a tongue at the passing level, your Presence trials in that tongue have Impotence. When speech is fast, technical, or full of wordplay, the GM may call for an Intelligence trial to follow it.
