# Creating a Character

## Rolling Attributes

Each attribute is assigned a set of three dice based on how important that attribute is to your class. Larger dice give higher scores.

| Dice Pool | Cost |
|:---:|:---:|
| 3d2 | 1 |
| 3d3 | 2 |
| 3d4 | 3 |
| 3d6 | 4 |
| 3d8 | 5 |
| 3d10 | 6 |
| 3d12 | 7 |

You have 36 points to spend on sets, one set for each attribute, such as:

* 3d3, 3d4, 3d6, 3d6, 3d8, 3d8, 3d10, and 3d12
* 3d4, 3d4, 3d6, 3d6, 3d8, 3d8, 3d10, and 3d10
* 3d3, 3d3, 3d3, 3d3, 3d12, 3d12, 3d12, and 3d12
* 3d6, 3d6, 3d6, 3d6, 3d8, 3d8, 3d8, and 3d8

Take your time in reviewing the suggested rolls below, especially in regard to skills and abilities that you might be interested in utilizing with a given class. If you want to play a fighter, but want to have a war dog companion, you might consider having a better *[Intelligence](#intelligence)*, which Wilderness uses to train animals.

* You can assign any set to any attribute, so long as you roll a minimum of 3dx per attribute.
* The suggested rolls below are not required, as you may consider Physique more important for your character than Intelligence, and swap the two rolls or similarly based on how you want your character to be built.
* Keep in mind that every attribute has a trial associated with it, in addition to other skills and abilities that make minimizing these attributes dangerous to your longevity.
* As you level, your attributes will improve, so if you did not roll particularly well, keep in mind that your are new to the adventuring career and these attributes will become better over time.

### What Your Attributes Do

Every attribute matters. Each one resists something that can take you out of a fight, and each one drives some of the weapons, magic, or skills you will lean on. A low score is a weakness an enemy can find.

<span id="strength"></span>**Strength.** Heavy and two-handed weapons, and the heavy thrown ones. How much you can carry. Breaking free of restraints, and resisting being held or pinned ([Restricted](../core/conditions.md#restricted "Strength trial to resist. Grappling, binding, entanglement, or physical constraint. Tiers compound. T1 — Grappled: Cannot move. Penalty -1 to physical rolls. T2 — Pinned: Cannot move. Penalty -2 to physical rolls. Inferiority on attack and defense rolls. T3 — Restrained: Cannot move or take any actions except attempts to resist. Considered Helpless. Can maintain concentration. Can cast spells without somatic components (+1 magan).")).

<span id="agility"></span>**Agility.** Your speed. Your armor resistance in light and medium armor. How fast your body reacts when combat opens (Startle). Dodging damaging spells, bursts, and falling debris.

<span id="physique"></span>**Physique.** Light weapons, thrown blades, and bows. Moving silently, climbing, balancing, swimming, and riding. Landing a fall, and resisting magic that would reshape or move your body.

<span id="endurance"></span>**Endurance.** Your health. Surviving when you fall to 0. Resisting poison, disease, and exhaustion ([Tainted](../core/conditions.md#tainted "Endurance trial to resist. Acute toxins, venom, or poisonous substances. Poison degrades body and mind. Tiers compound. T1 — Dosed: Penalty -1 to all rolls. T2 — Blighted: Penalty -2 to all rolls. Impotence on all rolls. Movement halved. Lose 1 health at end of turn. T3 — Corrupted: Penalty -3 to all rolls. Impotence on all rolls. Movement halved. Cannot take reactions. Lose 2 health at end of turn."), [Infected](../core/conditions.md#infected "Endurance trial to resist. Illness, disease, or supernatural contamination. Infected does not deteriorate naturally. At each incubation interval (determined by the disease), the creature makes an Endurance trial against the disease's DC. On success, the condition descends by 1 tier. On failure, the condition advances by 1 tier. If the condition descends below Tier 1, the disease is overcome. Tiers: Afflicted, Sickened, Diseased."), [Exhausted](../core/conditions.md#exhausted "Endurance trial to resist. Fatigue from lack of rest, overexertion, or environmental strain. Affects body and mind equally. Tiers compound. T1 — Weary: Impotence on all rolls. T2 — Fatigued: Impotence on all rolls. Inferiority on attack and defense rolls. All spells require miscast trials. T3 — Depleted: Impotence on all rolls. Inferiority on attack and defense rolls. All spells require miscast trials. Movement halved. 1 AP per round.")), and long days on the march.

<span id="intelligence"></span>**Intelligence.** The Occult tradition of the Bahador and Magus. Knowledge, crafts, and planning. Resisting confusion ([Disoriented](../core/conditions.md#disoriented "Intelligence trial to resist. Confusion, mental fog, or inability to process surroundings and act coherently. No dice modifiers at any tier. Loss of control is the punishment. Tiers: Befuddled, Confused, Bewildered.")) and magic that would read your mind.

<span id="wisdom"></span>**Wisdom.** The Telluric tradition of the Druid and Fili. Sound judgment. Resisting fear and charm ([Demoralized](../core/conditions.md#demoralized "Wisdom trial to resist. Fear directed at a specific source. While that source is in sight, the fear persists. While the source is out of sight, the creature makes the break-free trial at the end of each of its turns without spending AP. Tiers compound. Tiers: Shaken, Frightened, Panicked."), [Charmed](../core/conditions.md#charmed "Wisdom trial to resist. Magical or supernatural influence over disposition. The creature retains agency but its attitude toward the source is magically altered. Standard deterioration applies. Tiers: Lured, Beguiled, Enthralled.")).

<span id="charisma"></span>**Charisma.** The Theurgic tradition of the Avenger, Cleric, and Paladin. Persuasion, leadership, and disguise. Resisting control and possession ([Controlled](../core/conditions.md#controlled "Charisma trial to resist. Loss of bodily autonomy through magical domination or possession. Standard deterioration applies. Tiers: Compelled, Possessed, Dominated.")).

<span id="perception"></span>**Perception.** The Priscan tradition of the Cursed, Elves, and Gnome. Aiming every ranged attack. How fast your mind reacts when combat opens (Startle). Noticing what is hidden, and resisting being stunned or fooled by illusions ([Impaired](../core/conditions.md#impaired "Perception trial to resist. Stunning, disorientation, or delayed reflexes. Impaired from a failed Startle trial feeds into this chain. Tiers compound from T1 to T2; T3 transforms to total shutdown. T1 — Dazed: Penalty -1 to all rolls. Must act in Slow phase. T2 — Stunned: Penalty -2 to all rolls. Inferiority on attack rolls, defense rolls, and spell damage rolls. Must act in Slow phase. T3 — Paralyzed: Cannot take any actions. Considered Helpless and Catatonic. The creature is aware of its surroundings but unable to act or respond.")).

### Applying Points to Scores

After assigning a set of dice to a attribute, roll the assigned set, and check off a number of boxes equal to the points you rolled. It is expected that most of your rolls will result in Tier 2 or Tier 3 attributes. You may have 1 or 2 attributes at Tier 1 if you chose to make use of smaller dice sets. You will likely have no Tier 4 attributes, but it is not impossible.

All attributes fall on a scale from Tier 1 to Tier 7, with a Score between 1 and 30.

* Each score costs points to complete. Scores 1–5 cost 1 point each, 6–10 cost 2, 11–15 cost 3, 16–20 cost 4, 21–25 cost 5, and 26–30 cost 6. Completing the last score in a group gives you the next tier.

| Scores | Points each | Total to complete | Tier gained |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1–5 | 1 | 5 | 2 |
| 6–10 | 2 | 15 | 3 |
| 11–15 | 3 | 30 | 4 |
| 16–20 | 4 | 50 | 5 |
| 21–25 | 5 | 75 | 6 |
| 26–30 | 6 | 105 | 7 |

* You are considered to have the next Tier when you have completed the previous Tier (e.g. when you have a Score of 5, you are Tier 2, when you have a score of 30, you are Tier 7).

Before completing the process of crossing off your attributes, you can make a final change to these points to determine how your Scores will look.

* After generating your attributes, you may choose to EITHER swap any two scores or re-roll any 1 attribute.
* You gain 3 points to apply to any attributes: 1 point for being a level 1 character, and 2 starting points. The points from Leveling begin when you reach level 2.

#### Leveling

Each time you gain a level in your chosen class, you have a number of points equal to your level that you can apply to any attribute until you reach level 10, at which point you always gain 10 points per level (e.g. At level 4, you gain 4 points, at level 13, you gain 10 points, at level 32, you gain 10 points). There is no upper limit to leveling, although you will eventually max out all of your attributes, and that is probably a good stopping point.

<span id="beyond-level-30"></span>**Beyond Level 30.** Class tables end at level 30, but advancement continues. Each level beyond 30 requires 50 more experience than the level before it, grants 10 attribute points, and grants mastery points at your level 30 rate. Health continues on your class's schedule, and you keep your final title.

### Tier Dice Pools

Your health and your magan are pools of tier dice. Health follows your Endurance tier, and magan follows your casting attribute's tier. A pool holds one die for each tier up to your current tier: the Tier 1 die (d2), the Tier 2 die (d3), and so on. Record each die's result, so that you can reroll it later.

<span id="building-a-pool"></span>**Building a pool.** At character creation, build each pool one tier at a time. Roll the Tier 1 die. At each tier after it, roll that tier's die and add it to the pool, and you can reroll any dice already in the pool.

<span id="raising-a-pool"></span>**Raising a pool.** Each time the pool's attribute tier rises, roll the new tier die and add it to the pool. You can also reroll any dice already in the pool. A rerolled die keeps its new result, even if it is lower.

<span id="companions"></span>**Companions.** Companion health pools follow the same rules. A Puca's or an Agathion's pool rises with its tier, and an animal companion's rises with its handler's Wilderness mastery. A familiar adds its tier dice to the pool you share. A new or replacement companion builds its pool as at character creation.

<span id="variant-straight-rolls"></span>**Variant: Straight Rolls.** For a harder game, dice are never rerolled. At character creation, roll each die in the pool once.
